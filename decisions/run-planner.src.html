The short version

"Breaks the cap" means the line the run picked really hides more good tweets per 1,000 than your cap allows, judged against the simulated feed the run never saw. "Line wobble" is how far apart the 5th and 95th percentile picks land, in log-odds, averaged over the five mutes. 0 means every run picks the same line.

What the simulation can't know

Recommendation

Score 4,000, label about 900 in two stages

  1. Two-stage active labelling matches random 4,000 at under a quarter of the labelling. On the one-question design at the defaults, it breaks the 2-per-1,000 cap in 5% of runs with a line wobble of 0.35, using about 880 labels. Random 4,000 gets the same 5% and 0.35 but labels all 4,000. Both send 4,000 tweets to TypeSafe. That's about 4.8M Jev tokens if both designs are scored, and roughly 11 minutes end to end.
  2. Pick each line with a 90% upper bound, not the estimate. With the plain estimate, every strategy breaks the cap in 34–47% of runs, even at 4,000 labels. A line set exactly at the cap overshoots about as often as it undershoots. The upper bound brings that to 5–12% and costs almost no recall here.
  3. Label everything above 0.03, not 0.1. AI hype's right line sits near 0.05. With a 0.1 cutoff, its line rests on a thin random slice, and the smart strategy breaks the cap for that mute in 48% of runs.
  4. If 4,000 is too many to send, random 1,000 is a usable fallback. It breaks the cap in 9% of runs, with a worst case near 2.9 wrongly hidden per 1,000. But the rarest mute (crypto at 1%) gets only about 21 labelled examples, so its catch rate is known to about ±15 points. Don't let synthetic noise stand in for real: that mix breaks the cap in 14% of runs on one question and 41% on yes/no.

At messiness 0.8, a guess at how much rougher real language is, active slips to 10% and random 1,000's wobble nearly doubles. The real run will be closer to that end.

The one question I'd most want answered: Will you let 4,000 tweets from your feed go to TypeSafe for this run, or is 1,000 the ceiling? Everything else here is a setting I can choose, but that's your data.
