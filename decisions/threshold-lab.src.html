What it feels like in a scroll

Forty tweets drawn at the rates you set above, with every mute that's switched on, at each mute's current line. Real noise is rare, so most scrolls hide little. What you'll notice is whether a hide bar shows up where you'd have liked to read on. Bars don't say which hides were mistakes. You only find out by clicking Show, and most people never do.

Two ways of asking, same tweets

Each dot is one tweet scored both ways for the selected mute. Dots off the diagonal are where the two designs disagree. The table scores how cleanly each design separates noise from fine tweets (AUC: 1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is a coin flip), leaving debatable tweets out.

Which mutes are worth shipping

Each verdict uses the selected design. It finds the line that catches the most noise while wrongly hiding at most 2 good tweets per 1,000 (at your base-rate guesses), then checks how much of the mute's territory is debatable even to a careful reader.

What this page can't tell you yet

Recommendation

Ship one question per tweet, three mutes on by default, and treat dunking as a taste setting

  1. Ask one question per tweet, never a batch. Batching is settled: 11 of 40 borderline tweets switched sides depending only on their neighbours, while the same tweet asked alone twice barely moved.
  2. Default to the single "keep, or which mute?" question. On these tweets it hid 1 of 95 fine tweets at a 0.5 line. Yes/no per mute hid 3, and its dunking mute alone flagged 10 of 149 fine posts, mostly sharp opinions. The single question pays for that caution by missing about a third of the vague AI hype. That's the trade I'd take, because a wrong hide is invisible and a miss isn't.
  3. On by default: political rage bait, crypto shilling, engagement bait. Opt-in: vague AI hype. Not a default: dunking. Most of its territory is debatable even to me, so it should be a mute people add and tune themselves.
  4. Set lines from the real run, not from this page. With about 150 fine synthetic tweets per mute, the 90% range on "wrongly hidden per 1,000" spans roughly 0–20. The run needs thousands of real fine tweets to bring that under 2.
  5. Pin the model version and cap hides per mute per day, so a model update or a news cycle can't quietly change what you see.

The one question I'd most want answered: How much worse is one good tweet wrongly hidden than one piece of noise getting through: 5 times, 20 times, 100 times? That ratio sets every line, more than anything else on this page.
